# Harbourline audit: remaining robustness questions

Assessment date: 2 October 2026. Based on inspection of the current tests and implementation, after the first audit. No additional experiments or live provider calls were run for this assessment. These are coverage gaps and hypotheses, not newly reproduced bugs.

The subsequent audit, repairs and independent acceptance evidence are tracked in [the robustness progress report](2026-10-02-robustness-progress.md). This document retains the initial questions; that report records their current status and tested bounds.

## What the completed audit establishes

The 48 reported defects were repaired and independently rechecked. All 46 seeded playbooks complete for one supplied supported scenario each. The full automated suite and selected fuzz/race cases pass. The browser suite includes one complete prospect/director-appointment journey; many of its other 19 cases check layouts, permissions, editing or downloads.

The catalogue supplies scripted model answers and known-good facts, creates file/document fixtures directly, marks initial sending complete and waives payment, and completes external/manual actions with staff evidence fixtures. Separate tests exercise these boundaries, but they are not combined with every playbook and every possible ordering. Consequently this is not exhaustive customer-flow verification or a general guarantee of robustness under fuzzing.

## Priority scenarios still needing evidence

| Priority | Customer/staff scenario | Existing evidence | Remaining uncertainty and next test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Staff approve a reply or signing pack; the app/worker dies before completion, or the provider accepts it but the response is lost. | Approval gates, duplicate claims, selected exceptions and durable Xero receipt retry are covered. | Kill/restart at each boundary: claim, external acceptance, local acknowledgement, audit and job completion. Establish that nothing is lost, resent or permanently stranded, and expose a safe recovery action. `approveProposal` claims approved before its handler; `runJobs` claims running before its handler. Their exception handling does not establish recovery after a process dies. This is a concrete source-based concern, not a reproduced failure in this assessment. |
| 2 | Staff reply in Gmail while another browser approves Harbourline's draft for the same client. | Sequential mailbox capture withdraws drafts; actual PDF/link delivery evidence is tested. | Race outbound polling against approval, delayed Gmail visibility and duplicate/out-of-order messages. Require one coherent reply history, fresh approvals and no misleading delivery state. |
| 3 | A client requests several changes, partly pays, signs some documents, then cancels one change, adds another and changes the date. | Multi-request creation, partial cancellation before sending, issued-invoice preservation, stale approvals and individual date changes are tested. | Long mixed sequences crossing send, payment, signing and filing boundaries. Verify current fees, document packs, upload links, pending cards and statutory records together; establish the correction/escalation path after an irreversible external action. |
| 4 | Two tasks change the same company's board, shares or controllers; one is backdated, or the same person serves several companies. | Effective-date membership, a backdated overdraw, individual effects, atomic multi-transfer rollback and a cancellation/approval race are tested. | Concurrent independent approvals, same-day ordering, reappointments, several share classes/currencies, subsequent backdated changes and shared-person edits. Check all historical dates and prevent changes from contaminating another company's work. Historical person particulars already have a documented limitation. |
| 5 | Two signers finish together while staff change or withdraw the pack; OTP requests and guesses arrive concurrently. | Sequential multi-signer completion, OTP expiry/attempt limits, withdrawn links, malformed PNGs and storage failure are tested. | Simultaneous last signatures, partial provider delivery of signing invitations, withdraw/sign interleavings, concurrent OTP resends/attempt counts, browser retries and expiry boundaries. Require consistent documents, certificates and retry behavior. |
| 6 | A client makes one bank transfer for several invoices, overpays, pays net of fees, or a deposit appears late and out of order. | Exact/reference matching, partial receipts, stale/currency checks, selected concurrent receipts and Xero lost-response reconciliation are tested with fakes. | Real bank pagination/cursor/reconciliation behavior and complex payment allocation. The current receipt path permits one use of a bank transaction, so split allocation is not established. Refunds/credit notes are explicitly unbuilt; test how unsupported cases are surfaced and resolved rather than claiming they work. |
| 7 | The real model reads ambiguous bilingual instructions or hostile content embedded in an email/PDF. | Scripted model responses exercise schema and domain safeguards; a separate live evaluation harness exists. | Prompt injection, valid-but-wrong person/client/intent selection, contradictions across threads, poor scans and repeated model variability. Test redacted realistic fixtures and separately controlled live evaluations. Passing a supplied model answer does not establish model comprehension or extraction accuracy. |
| 8 | A client submits real scans and long names/addresses, then asks for a full filing pack. | Selected PDF fields, template language rules, file-size boundaries, malformed signatures and document generation are tested. | Corrupt/large/mislabelled files, rotated or multipage scans, extreme image dimensions, OCR errors, continuation pages and long CJK text. Visually and semantically inspect the resulting documents, not only their existence, template IDs or page counts. Statutory acceptance still requires professional verification. |
| 9 | A secretary edits a playbook and a running task combines the revised workflow with holds, third-party steps and later costs. | Immutable version pinning, invalid definitions, stale editor protection and individual hold/cost/third-party rules are tested. | Valid but unusual definitions, optional requirements, reordered gates, and long mixed sequences. Require no skipped approval, stuck task, misleading chase or rewritten issued invoice. The 46 seeded definitions are not a fuzz corpus of edited workflows. |
| 10 | A firm runs thousands of tasks for months: deadlines roll over, the client uploads during escalation, and held work is released. | Selected deadline, recurrence, chase, escalation, hold and demo-clock rules are tested. | Soak/load tests, HK-midnight/year/leap boundaries, worker downtime, overdue backlog, simultaneous upload/chase/hold changes and UI performance on realistic data volumes. No production load benchmark or long-running worker recovery test was established. |

## Deferred capabilities

Login/roles/RLS and deployment isolation, live Gmail/Airwallex/Xero behavior, TPSI filing, official WeChat integration, trial-data import/export/deletion and backup restoration were not certified by this audit. Some are expressly deferred or unbuilt. Tests of domain firm predicates do not prove production tenant isolation. These should have separate acceptance gates before real design-partner data is introduced.

## Next audit order

Start with process-death/provider-acknowledgement recovery, then simultaneous mailbox/approval/signing actions, then generated sequences of amendments/cancellations/payments/holds across multiple tasks. Those expand coverage beyond one scenario per playbook and a fixed invalid-input corpus. Follow with real-document/model evaluation and controlled provider sandbox tests. Preserve exact reproduction seeds and business-state invariants, and distinguish unsupported procedures from implementation defects.
